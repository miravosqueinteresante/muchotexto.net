# Economic Context for Agricultural Tokenization in Paraguay

## 1. Agricultural Sector Share of GDP

- **Agriculture, forestry, and fishing value added as % of GDP (2024): 10.71%** (down from 11.59% in 2023)
- The **primary sector** (agriculture + livestock + other activities) contributed approximately **10.71% of GDP** in 2024
- The sector employs **~17.17% of the labor force**
- In 2025, agricultural GDP grew **10.5%** year-on-year, driven by strong corn, wheat, rice, and cotton production, partially offset by lower soybean output
- Paraguay's economy is highly dependent on agriculture: **80% of direct exports** and at least 17% of output rely on agriculture, livestock, and hydropower
- Long-term trend: agriculture's share has declined from ~38% in the 1960s to ~10.7% today, but remains structurally significant

## 2. Land Ownership Structure

### CAN 2022 (VI Censo Agropecuario Nacional) Key Findings:

- **291,497 farms** covering **30,401,660 hectares** total
- **97% of farms** in the Eastern Region (283,052 farms), **3%** in the Western Region/Chaco (8,445 farms)
- Eastern Region: **44% of surface area** (13.4M ha); Western Region: **56%** (17.0M ha)

### Extreme Land Concentration:

- **40% of agricultural land is held by 0.07% of landowners** — near-perfect inequality scenario
- Gini coefficient essentially unchanged between 2008 and 2022, indicating **persistent, structural concentration**
- **Large producers (>500 ha in Eastern Region):** only **4,301 farms** but control **~8.9 million hectares** (~29% of total)
- **Medium producers:** ~17,267 farms with ~2.5 million hectares
- **Small producers (<20 ha):** the most numerous but their total area has been shrinking — they hold only **~8.3% of agricultural land** (2.5M ha)

### Farm Size Evolution (2008–2022):

- Farms of **<1 ha** grew **35.5%** in area (to 9,340 ha) — more smallholders in less land
- Farms of **1-10 ha** decreased in number (from 187,172 to 184,221)
- Farms of **20-50 ha** grew **13.5%** in number
- Farms of **50-100 ha** grew **26.7%** in number
- Large farms (>5,000 ha) decreased in number and area

### Land Tenure:

- **55%** of farms have definitive title (160,273 farms)
- **29%** have provisional documentation
- **22%** operate under rental, sharecropping, or other forms
- **95.4%** of farms managed by individual producers; **1.5%** by companies/state/indigenous communities
- **96.7%** of producers are Paraguayan nationals; Brazilian nationals account for **1.59%**

## 3. Access to Credit for Small and Medium Agricultural Producers

### Current Situation:

- **Only 18% of agricultural producers obtained credit** (2006-2007 baseline, World Bank)
- Only **22% of Paraguayan adults** borrowed from formal financial institutions (Global Findex 2025)
- Small producers have the **lowest access** to credit, technical assistance, inputs, and machinery
- **Credit access increases with farm size** — a structural barrier for smallholders

### Key Barriers (farmer-reported):

| Barrier | % citing it |
|---|---|
| High interest rates | 68% |
| Information requirements | 65% |
| Physical distance to bank | 57% |

### Institutional Credit Programs:

- **Crédito Agrícola de Habilitación (CAH):** Public institution mandated to serve micro and small agricultural producers. Offers rates of **10% (productive investment)** to **18% (standard products)**. Maximum financing: 25 minimum wages (~Gs. 68M).
- **FOGAPY (Fondo de Garantía del Paraguay):** Guarantee fund backing loans up to Gs. 750M at **11.5% annual interest**, up to 7-year terms.
- **AFD Pro Campo:** Lines for reproductive cattle and agricultural investment.

### International Development Programs:

- **World Bank PIMA** ($100M, 2020): Market Access for Agricultural Products — benefits ~170,000 small/medium farmers and indigenous producers
- **World Bank PRODERS** ($137.5M total): Sustainable Agriculture and Rural Development — benefited 249,662 small-scale farmers
- **IDB PR-L1170** ($ commitment): Access to Financing for Agricultural Investments — medium and long-term financing for technology
- **OPEC Fund** ($50M to Banco Continental, 2023): On-lending to agri SMEs for food security
- **IDB Invest — Bancop** ($20M): Food security financing partnership for small/medium agri producers
- **IDB Invest — LAR** ($10M): Agricultural value chain strengthening

## 4. Current Interest Rates for Agricultural Loans

### BCP Policy Rate:

- **Monetary policy rate: 6.00%** (as of late 2024–2025, down from 6.25%)

### System-wide Average Rates (BCP Financial Indicators):

| Category | MN (Guaraníes) | ME (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| **Average active rate** (Nov 2025) | **16.04%** | **7.92%** |
| Commercial loans (May 2025) | **11.21%** | — |
| Development loans (May 2025) | **8.90%** | — |
| Consumer loans (May 2025) | **21.54%** | — |
| Credit cards (Jan 2025) | **15.10%** | — |
| Housing (Jan 2025) | **10.27%** | — |
| Average passive rate | 5–6% | ~3.8% |

### CAH (Agricultural Credit) Specific Rates:

- **Productive Investment:** **10%** annual (up to 7-year terms)
- **Productive Investment 2.0 (FOGAPY):** **11.5%** annual
- **Standard products (PROAGRO, Mercadeo):** **18%** annual
- **MAG-prioritized crops:** **10%** annual (investment), **14%** for operating/commercialization costs
- **EPA RE Inversión:** **10%** annual

### Commercial Bank Agricultural Products (example: Bancop):

- **PYMES Agrícola:** up to USD 100,000, max 365-day term, 1% disbursement fee
- **Maquinarias:** up to 5-year terms
- **Ganadería Segura (with FOGAPY):** up to Gs. 1 billion, up to 5-year terms
- **Reproductores (with AFD Pro Campo):** up to 8-year terms

### Trend:

- Rates in MN have been trending down slowly (from ~15.5% in Jan 2024 to ~14% in mid-2025)
- Wide spread between policy rate (6%) and lending rates (11–22%) indicates **high intermediation costs and risk premiums**
- Foreign currency loans significantly cheaper but carry exchange rate risk

## 6. Financial Inclusion Rates in Rural Paraguay

### Global Findex 2025 Key Data (World Bank):

| Indicator | Paraguay |
|---|---|
| **Adults with a financial account** | **61%** |
| Saved at a formal institution | **20%** |
| Borrowed from formal institution | **22%** |
| Made/received digital payments | **55%** |
| Own a mobile phone | **94%** |
| Used internet recently | **84%** |

### ASOBAN/BCP Data (2024, broader definition):

- **79% of adult population** (3.7 million clients) have at least one financial product
- **21% growth** in clients year-over-year (658,730 new clients in 2024)
- **151% growth** since 2015 (from 1.4M to 3.7M)
- **9.8 million deposit accounts** (up 36.2% year-over-year)
- **49.8% of clients are women**

### Rural Access Gap:

- **30% of districts** (69 of 224) with >2,000 inhabitants have **zero bank presence** (no branch, agent, or ATM)
- **17% of the population** lives in areas with **no bank or cooperative branches**
- Rural areas have **limited access to formal financial services**
- Two-thirds of the extreme poor are in rural areas

### Cooperative Sector:

- **800+ cooperatives** with **1.8 million members**
- Represents **51% of the economically active population**
- Key alternative to traditional banking, especially in rural areas
- 28% of adults use mobile money; 21% have accounts/loans with cooperatives; 15% with banks
- DGRV works with INCOOP and MAG to strengthen cooperative financial services

## 7. Previous Agricultural Financing Innovation in Paraguay

### Mobile Factoring (CAH + World Bank, 2013–2014):

- First proof of concept of **mobile factoring** for small agricultural producers
- Implemented through **Crédito Agrícola de Habilitación (CAH)** with World Bank technical assistance
- Producers could request credit against confirmed credit sales via an **electronic platform on mobile phones**
- Two cooperatives tested: Cooperativa Guayaivi Unido (95 members) and Cooperativa Pacova Poty (90 members)
- Average credit of ~40 days based on banana sales to CEPACOOP
- Each credit directly benefited ~20 people
- Limitations: electronic invoices and signatures were not yet implemented in Paraguay, requiring hybrid paper-digital processes
- Funded in partnership with **JICA (Japan International Cooperation Agency)**

### Warrant Agricultural / Descuento de Documento:

- CAH offers **"Crédito a Través del Descuento de Documento (CDD)"** — a warrant-style product
- CAH/Mercadeo: financing for harvest labor, inputs, transport, storage while waiting for better prices
- These are precursor models to tokenized warehouse receipts

### Cooperatives and Agricultural Finance:

- **800+ cooperatives**, 1.8M members (51% of EAP)
- **DGRV** works with INCOOP and MAG on:
  - Credit guarantee funds for cooperatives
  - Sustainable Finance Initiative
  - SiCoop digital innovation initiative
  - CERECOOP strengthening for member cooperatives
- **IDB — Cooperativa Naranjito ($1.6M):** Precision agriculture for small/medium farmers
- **IDB Invest — LAR ($10M):** Agricultural inputs and grains, 80% of purchases from small/medium producers

### Public Credit Infrastructure:

- **CAH** (Crédito Agrícola de Habilitación): Public bank serving ~micro and small agri producers
  - Founded under Law 551/75, reformed by Law 5361/2020
  - Authorized capital: Gs. 228.9 billion
  - Wide product range: 20+ products for different segments
  - Guaranteed by the State
- **FOGAPY (Fondo de Garantía del Paraguay):** Guarantee fund enabling lending to smaller producers
- **AFD (Agencia Financiera de Desarrollo):** Second-tier development bank funding agricultural investment

### Tokenization Developments:

- **May 2026:** "Paraguay opens doors to agricultural tokenization" (ABC Color) — first major media signal
- DeFi Producer Cooperatives: emerging model combining cooperative governance with DeFi mechanisms for smallholder credit access
- Tokenization of cattle, land, grains, and agricultural contracts on blockchain being explored

### BCP Crisis Management for Agriculture:

- **Recurring transitory measures** (2024, 2025) for drought-affected producers:
  - Loan refinancing without penalty
  - Extended repayment periods
  - Suspension of delinquency counting
  - These signal structural vulnerability to climate shocks and the need for better risk management tools

### IDB/World Bank Programs:

- **IDB PR-L1170** (2020): Access to medium and long-term financing for agricultural technology adoption
- **World Bank PRODERS** (2009–2020): $137.5M, 249,662 beneficiaries
- **World Bank PIMA** (2020): $100M, 170,000 beneficiaries
- **OPEC Fund** (2023): $50M to Banco Continental for agri SMEs

## Summary: Tokenization Potential

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Agricultural GDP share | 10.7% — significant but declining |
| Land concentration | Extreme: 40% of land in 0.07% of owners |
| Credit gap | Only 18% producers financed; high rates (10–22%) |
| Rural financial inclusion | 61% account ownership; 30% of districts have zero bank presence |
| Export dependence | Soy alone = 30% of exports; vulnerable to price/climate shocks |
| Existing innovation | Mobile factoring, warrant products, cooperative finance, guarantee funds |
| Enabling infra | 94% mobile ownership, 84% internet, 55% digital payments |
| Policy direction | Tokenization being explored (2026); digital finance growing rapidly |

**Key opportunity:** Paraguay has the **raw economic drivers** (large agricultural GDP, credit gap, high digital adoption) and **precedent models** (mobile factoring, warrant lending, cooperatives) that make agricultural tokenization viable. The main challenges are **extreme land inequality**, **climate vulnerability**, and **high intermediation costs** — all of which tokenization could potentially address through fractionalization, transparent title tracking, and disintermediated financing.
