## 2. Digital Payments Infrastructure

### Instant Payment System (SPI)

- **Launched:** May 23, 2022, by Banco Central del Paraguay (BCP).
- **Functionality:** 24/7 electronic fund transfers, free of charge, funds available in <1 minute.
- **Growth:** Sipap averaged 560,000 daily transactions by August 2024 (4x increase from 141,000 pre-SPI).
- **Projected:** 172 million+ transactions by end of 2025 (113% increase since 2023).
- **June 2025:** Nearly 28 million transactions in a single month (more than all of 2022 combined).
- **Impact:** 2+ million new accounts created related to SPI usage.
- **Technology:** CMA Small Systems AB (Sweden) IPS solution.

### National Payment System Law (Law 7503)

- **Promulgated:** June 2025.
- Establishes solid legal and regulatory framework for payment ecosystem supervision.
- Recognized by World Bank as formal prior action for Development Policy Loan.

### QR Payment Adoption

- **64% of Paraguayans already pay with QR** — even in public streets.
- National QR standards being developed to create interoperable merchant payments framework.

### Card Transactions

- 13.3 card transactions per second in Paraguay.
- Card transactions grew 5x vs 2021.
- Total card purchases: Gs. 55.8 trillion.

## 3. Banking Penetration & Financial Inclusion

### Global Findex 2025 (World Bank)

- **Account ownership:** 61% of Paraguayan adults (age 15+) — consistent progress since 2011.
- **Digital payments:** Primary driver of the account ownership growth — has doubled financial inclusion.
- **Mobile money:** Decisive factor in expanding inclusion.
- **Context:** Paraguay went from 51% (global 2011) to 79% (global 2024) but still below regional leaders.
- **Gender gap:** Narrowing but still present.

### BCP Data

- Banking system remains stable and solvent (IMF, 2024 review).
- Interest rate: 27.05% (usuraria rate in national currency).
- New SIP brand identity launched April 2026 for payment system modernization.

### World Bank Digital Finance Portal

- Paraguay shows formal saving, borrowing, and digital payments adoption growing.
- Electronic money issuers (EMIs) primarily focused on cash-in/cash-out — untapped potential for fully digital services.

## 4. Energy Abundance

### Itaipú Binacional

- **World's 2nd largest hydroelectric plant** by annual production (3rd largest ever built).
- **2025 total generation:** 72,879 GWh (+8.6% vs 2024).
- **2025 supply to ANDE:** 25,768 GWh (+26.4% vs 2024) — new record.
- **January-April 2026 supply to ANDE:** 10,170 GWh (+24.3% vs same period 2025).
- **Availability factor:** 96.29% in 2025 (exceeding 94% target).
- **Total historical generation:** 3,124,679 GWh since 1984.
- **Royalties transferred to Paraguay in 2025:** USD 462 million.

### Yacyretá (EBY)

- **2025 gross annual generation:** 20,000 GWh (20 million MWh).
- November 2025: historic monthly peak exceeding 2 million MWh.
- Data transparency issues: EBY has restricted access to energy data since new administration.

### ANDE (National Electricity Administration)

- **2025 national consumption:** 29,419 GWh (+12.5% vs 2024) — 2nd highest increase in decade.
- Supply breakdown: Itaipú 87.6%, Yacyretá 10.5%, Acaray 1.9%.
- **Historical max demand:** 5,280 MW (Dec 15, 2025).
- **Installed capacity:** 8.83 GW hydro (2024).

### Energy Costs

- **Industrial: ~$0.037–0.052/kWh** (Bitfarms PPA: 3.9 cents/kWh, before VAT, not subject to inflation adjustments).
- **Residential: ~$0.062/kWh** (PYG 401.995/kWh).
- **Business: ~$0.052/kWh** (PYG 334.798/kWh).
- Paraguay electricity prices are **32% of world average residential**, **27% of world average business**.
- **100% renewable** — hydroelectric.
- Paraguay consumes <25% of Itaipu's total output; **~32 TWh annual surplus**.
- Surplus historically exported to Brazil at ~$10/MWh; miners offer **$40/MWh** (4x export price).

## 5. Cryptocurrency Mining in Paraguay

### Scale

- Paraguay ranks **#4 globally** in Bitcoin mining hash rate: **~43 EH/s, 4.3% of global hashrate** (Q2 2026).
- **45 licensed crypto mining companies** (2024), 20+ additional applicants.
- Total proposed capacity from applicants: ~2,000 MW.
- Projected ANDE revenue from mining: **$48 million (2024) → $125 million (2026)**.

### Major Operations

| Company | Capacity | Location | Details |
|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| **HIVE Digital** | 300 MW (expanding to 400 MW) | Yguazú | 8.5 BTC/day; completed Phase 1 (Apr 2025), Phase 2 (Sep 2025); expanding to full 400 MW by Q3 2026. Total target: 35 EH/s globally. Owns 240 MVA substation. |
| **Bitfarms (now Keel Infrastructure)** | Up to 150 MW | Villarrica (50 MW) + Yguazú (100 MW) | PPA at ~3.9 cents/kWh. |
| **Marathon Digital Holdings** | 27 MW | Near Itaipu Dam | Joint venture with Penguin Infrastructure Holding, launched Nov 2023. |

### Legal & Regulatory Status

- **Licensing:** Administered by Ministry of Industry and Commerce.
- **Tax exemption:** Mining energy use exempt from taxes until 2027 (for regulated operations only).
- **Ban attempt:** April 2024 bill proposing 180-day mining ban; Senator Salyn Buzarquis led suspension.
- **DNIT Resolution 47/2026:** Mandatory reporting for crypto transactions >$5,000/year — wallet addresses, blockchain networks, transaction hashes.
- **Securities Law (Law 7572/2025):** Formalizes oversight of tokenized assets via Securities Superintendency (SIV).
- **Tax treatment (Consulta Vinculante N.582, Oct 2024):** Crypto classified as a "good"; natural persons NOT subject to IRE/IRP on capital gains; companies subject to 10% IRE; crypto sales not subject to VAT.

### Illegal Mining

- ANDE estimates: 72 legal firms vs unknown illegal operations.
- Each illegal mining instance causes ~$94,900 in damages; annual losses potentially $60 million.
- Maximum sentence for energy theft: 10 years.
- 10,000+ ASICs and 50 transformers seized in 2024.

## 6. Crypto Adoption (Chainalysis Index)

### Latin America Regional Context

- Latin America received **~$1.5 trillion in crypto transaction volume** (July 2022–June 2025).
- **63% YoY growth** in crypto adoption in Latin America (2025).
- Peak: $87.7 billion in December 2024 (single month).
- Stablecoins dominate usage — driven by inflation hedging, remittances, and cross-border payments.
- Brazil ranks **#5 globally** in Chainalysis 2025 Adoption Index (highest in Latin America).

### Paraguay's Position

- Paraguay does NOT appear in the Chainalysis top-20 global adoption index (which is dominated by India, US, Pakistan, Vietnam, Brazil, etc.).
- However, Paraguay ranks **#4 globally in Bitcoin mining hashrate** (~43 EH/s, 4.3% of global) — institutional/industrial adoption far exceeds retail adoption.
- The disconnect: Paraguay is a **mining powerhouse** but still emerging in retail/grassroots crypto adoption.
- Tokenization projects (e.g., Asunción Innovation Valley at $6M on Polkadot) signal growing blockchain interest.

## 7. Tokenization & Blockchain Initiatives

### Asunción Innovation Valley (AIV)

- **Value:** $6 million equity tokenization on Polkadot (Moonbeam parachain).
- **Structure:** 130,000 equity tokens backed by land value of new Innovation Valley district (hotel, convention center, university, data center).
- **Platform:** Better Use Blockchain (Brazilian), integrated with Polkadot.
- **Developer:** Paradata (blockchain development firm).
- **Status:** First tranche distributed to founding partners; full go-live expected Q3 2025.
- **Future:** Second tranche in 2028; profit-sharing and voting rights for token holders.
- **Regulatory context:** Compliant with Paraguayan securities law under Law 7572/2025.

### Paraguay Blockchain Summit

- Held August 2025 in Asunción.
- Emphasis on public-private collaboration for national blockchain agenda.
- Paraguay positioning as regional blockchain hub leveraging:
  - 100% renewable energy supply
  - Young and skilled workforce
  - Favorable tax environment

### Recent Developments

- **Internet exchange points:** 3 active IXPs.

### Digital Identity (Identidad Electrónica)

- **Developed by:** MITIC (Ministerio de Tecnologías de la Información y Comunicación).
- **Launch:** Since 2023; data available at datos.gov.py.
- **Function:** Unique digital ID for online government procedures; constitutes electronic signature (Law 4.017/13).
- **Scope:** 400+ online procedures available via Portal Paraguay (paraguay.gov.py).
- **International:** Presented at XIX Reunión Interamericana de Gobierno Digital (Oct 2025); part of regional identity broker project (Red GEALC).
- **Expat access:** May 2026 — exceptional procedure enabled for Paraguayans in Spain with expired IDs.
- **Digitalia (e-filing system):** 13,000+ digital files processed, saving 1+ ton of paper.

## 9. Comparative Assessment: Readiness for Blockchain-Based Financial Innovation

### Strengths

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Energy** | World-class advantage. 100% renewable, cheapest industrial rates in Latin America (~$0.04/kWh), ~32 TWh annual surplus. Supports energy-intensive blockchain applications. |
| **Crypto Mining** | Already #4 globally in Bitcoin hashrate. Established regulatory framework for mining. Tax exemption until 2027. |
| **Payments Infrastructure** | Modern SPI instant payment system; 64% QR adoption; Law 7503 provides legal framework. BCP won Central Banking award 2025 for payments infrastructure. |
| **Digital Identity** | MITIC's Identidad Electrónica operational; 400+ online procedures; recognized regionally. |
| **Tax Environment** | Territorial tax system; crypto gains not taxed for individuals; 10% corporate rate. Crypto classified as "good" — favorable treatment. |
| **Tokenization** | Law 7572/2025 regulates tokenized assets. First major project (AIV, $6M) underway on Polkadot. |
| **Political Will** | Peña administration pro-foreign investment; pro-crypto mining stance despite bumps. |

### Weaknesses / Risks

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Fintech Maturity** | Only 34% LATAM maturity index; 38-105 companies (small ecosystem vs Brazil/Mexico). |
| **Retail Crypto Adoption** | Low grassroots adoption vs regional peers; absent from Chainalysis top-20. |
| **Data Protection** | No comprehensive data protection law — gap for DeFi/blockchain KYC compliance. |
| **Internet in Rural Areas** | Only ~50% rural coverage (though improving via Starlink). |
| **Banking Penetration** | 61% account ownership — 39% still unbanked. Mobile money underutilized beyond cash-in/out. |
| **Regulatory Uncertainty** | Mining-ban attempts (though defeated); pagaré digital and Ley de Servicios de Confianza not fully implemented; DNIT crypto reporting rules in flux. |
| **Grid Strain** | 2,000 MW in mining applications risks grid stability; ANDE struggling with illegal mining. |
| **Data Transparency** | Yacyretá energy data locked; limited public reporting from some state entities. |

### Key Opportunities for Blockchain Innovation

1. **Tokenization of real-world assets** (real estate, agricultural commodities, energy credits) — AIV sets precedent.
2. **Stablecoin-based payments** for the 39% unbanked — leveraging existing QR infrastructure + SPI rails.
3. **Energy-backed tokens** — monetize Itaipú surplus via blockchain-based energy certificates or mining tokens.
4. **Cross-border remittances** — Paraguay's territorial tax system + stablecoins = natural corridor.
5. **Agricultural supply chain finance** — tokenize soy, beef, and other commodity receipts for working capital.
